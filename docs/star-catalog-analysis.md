# Cross-Cultural Fixed Star Comparison

## Methodology

Three traditions, three star catalogs:

1. **Western (Ptolemy)**: Tetrabiblos Book I, Chapters IX-XI. Every constellation assigned planetary natures. Individual bright stars named with specific natures (Regulus=Mars+Jupiter, Spica=Venus+Mars, etc.).

2. **Vedic**: Nakshatra system (27 lunar mansions) covers ecliptic-proximate stars. Off-ecliptic stars appear in Brihat Samhita and Puranic texts (Agastya/Canopus, Lubdhaka/Sirius, Brahmahridaya/Capella, Abhijit/Vega).

3. **Chinese**: 28 xiu (lunar mansions) cover ecliptic. San Yuan (Three Enclosures) cover northern circumpolar. Individual stars named and interpreted in Shi Shen's star catalog and Kaiyuan Zhanjing.

## Analysis

**Stars with genuine 3-way interpretive agreement (off-ecliptic):**
- Algol: malefic/death (Western, Chinese, Arabic)
- Sirius: martial/bright/dangerous (Western, Vedic, Chinese)
- Vega: artistic/creative (Western, Vedic, Chinese)
- Capella: leadership/authority (Western, Vedic, Chinese)
- Canopus: wisdom/old age (Western, Vedic, Chinese)

**Stars where cultures disagree:**
- Fomalhaut: Western=creative, Chinese=martial
- Procyon: Western=quick+martial, Chinese=river/flow

**Stars with no cross-cultural data (too far south):**
- Achernar, Agena, Acrux

## Recommendation

Add 5 off-ecliptic stars (Tier 2) to the 20 mansion stars = **25-star catalog**.

These 5 have the same kind of signal as the mansion overlap: multiple cultures independently arrived at similar interpretations. The difference is the mansion overlap is structural (same star used as calendar anchor), while the interpretive agreement is semantic (same star described with similar qualities). Both are cross-cultural signal.

The 5 additions are all first magnitude or brighter (Algol at 2.12 is the dimmest, but its variability makes it functionally brighter — it's the most famous variable star in the sky).
